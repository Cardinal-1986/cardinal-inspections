Cardinal — Landing & Retail. three directions

Every number below is real, queried 30 Aug 2026 — 60 clients (retail 23, community 16, insurance 2, 19 filed under no CRM), 3 community bids past due, 5 open urgent punch-outs, 2 claims, nothing on today's calendar. The zero-state is shown on purpose. Icons here are placeholders for the real cardinal-transparent.png / cardinal-logo-insurance.svg marks.

Direction 1The Brief — keep the editorial landing, add the state layer

The least disruptive of the three. The Georgia-serif landing stays exactly as it is; build 1048's chip strip moves up onto it and grows a Community chip; each of the three courses gains a live state line under its subtitle. Emoji become Cardinal marks. A three-item utility row (you / settings / sign out) replaces the missing menu — the landing is the menu, so a burger inside it would be a menu inside a menu.

Cost: retail only gets an accent, so it is still the quietest of the three CRMs. Buys: the smallest diff, no layout risk, and it is the only direction that could ship in one build.

Direction 2The Board — three columns, real rows, not counts

The landing becomes a board: one column per CRM, each showing the actual work waiting rather than a number. You read names on the front door. Stacks to three cards on a phone, spreads to three columns on the ultrawide — which is the screen this direction is really for. The retail home narrows to Pipeline + Today + AR; the KP cards, activity strip and month calendar move behind a More row.

Cost: the biggest layout change of the three, and the phone version is a long scroll. Buys: the most information per screen, and it is the only direction where you can see a specific overdue bid without tapping anything.

Direction 3The Desk — one list, ordered by urgency, CRM as a colour

The landing stops being three compartments and becomes one prioritised list of everything waiting on you, each row stamped with its CRM's colour. The three portal doors drop underneath as a quiet strip. This is exactly how api/digest.js already composes the morning email — so for the first time the screen and the email would say the same thing in the same order. And because the landing is now CRM-neutral by construction, retail is free to take a full identity on its own home screen.

Cost: the most opinionated, and it demotes "which CRM am I in" — which may be wrong for Curtis and Scottie, who never see this screen anyway (build 854). Buys: one place that answers "what do I do next", and it never shows an empty compartment.
