Direction 1The Brief — keep the editorial landing, add the state layer

The least disruptive of the four. The Georgia-serif landing stays exactly as it is; build 1048's chip strip moves up onto it and grows a Community chip; each of the three courses gains a live state line under its subtitle. Emoji become Cardinal marks. The awaiting-contract chip opens the retail home at its Approvals card — 1048's door, given a landing address. A three-item utility row (you / settings / sign out) replaces the missing menu — the landing is the menu, so a burger inside it would be a menu inside a menu.

Cost: retail only gets an accent, so it is still the quietest of the three CRMs. Buys: the smallest diff, no layout risk, and it is the only direction that could ship in one build.

Direction 2The Board — three columns, real rows, not counts

The landing becomes a board: one column per CRM, each showing the actual work waiting rather than a number. You read names on the front door. Stacks to three cards on a phone, spreads to three columns on the ultrawide — which is the screen this direction is really for. The retail home narrows to Pipeline + Today + AR; the KP cards, activity strip and month calendar move behind a More row.

Cost: the biggest change to the home's own layout, and the phone version is a long scroll. Buys: the most information per screen, and it is the only direction where you can see a specific overdue bid without tapping anything.

Direction 3The Desk — one list, ordered by urgency, CRM as a colour

The landing stops being three compartments and becomes one prioritised list of everything waiting on you, each row stamped with its CRM's colour. The three portal doors drop underneath as a quiet strip. It is the closest of the four to how api/digest.js already assembles the morning email — the same signals on one surface (the email still groups them by kind) — so the screen and the email finally carry the same facts. And because the landing is now CRM-neutral by construction, retail is free to take a full identity on its own home screen.

Cost: the most opinionated, and it demotes "which CRM am I in" — which may be wrong for Curtis and Scottie, who never see this screen anyway (build 854). Buys: one place that answers "what do I do next", and it never shows an empty compartment.

Direction 4The Switcher — no front door; the portal lives in the header

This is what the concept render actually drew: no Landing at all. The header carries Retail ⌄, tapping it drops the portal switcher, and every row in that dropdown is a door with a live state line — so "what's going on everywhere" is one tap from any screen instead of a screen of its own. The dashboard is then honestly retail-filtered. Today's home says Retail while counting all three CRMs — the concept's "37 leads / 11 proposals" are the all-CRM totals; retail alone is 10 and 7. This direction is the one that makes the label true. It also adopts the concept's grouped left nav on desktop (CRM / Jobs / Sales / Finance / Resources / Reports / System — the concept's grouping, real destinations only), and fixes the AR card so the big number is the money owed, not the chart's axis ceiling. Refined after Theo's screenshots: while the switcher is open the header stops saying Retail and reads Cardinal — an open picker claims no portal, 1087's rule — and the open panel takes the Brief's editorial treatment: the greeting, the date, and the app's own daily quote (the shipped landing already rotates 31 of them by day-of-year; today's really is the Lincoln), so nothing the Landing did is lost when the Landing goes. Production is already a row in the dropdown, with its punch count. The dashboard gains the 30-day calendar, fed by the same cacheAppts window the outlook circle already reads, and keeps the shipped home's Client Projects card — Most Recent and Nearby, the two rows Theo asked to hold onto — now honestly retail-filtered. Name for the open panel — numbered picks: 1. Cardinal. (shown) · 2. “Where things stand” as the title · 3. “Home Base”.

Cost: deletes the Landing — the morning brief loses its full-screen moment and the only all-CRM view becomes a dropdown behind one tap. Sign-in lands per role (Production already lands on its hub, build 854). Buys: zero screens between opening the app and working, the header always says where you are, and it is the closest to your reviewer's picture. Note 1087 is not re-crossed: the Landing could not claim a portal because it is where you choose one — here every screen genuinely is inside a portal.
