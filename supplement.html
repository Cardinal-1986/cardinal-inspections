Code authority

Building-official letters, kept by jurisdiction. Ohio has one residential code — a city or county does not write its own, it runs the office that issues the permit. So a letter is not a second citation; it is proof of how the RCO section lands at this address, and a carrier cannot fund an installation the jurisdiction will not permit. A letter filed here answers every future job in that jurisdiction.

What the scope is missing

Nothing is pre-ticked — every item on the letter is your call. Citations come from Cardinal's own Supplement Templates; the AI cannot invent one. Quantities marked "measured" were computed from this job's measurements, not by the model. Each item shows its three legs — a photograph, a quantity and an authority. Carriers deny on documentation more often than on the damage itself, so an item missing a photograph or a number is held back until it has one, or until you override it.

What you know that the scope does not say

Anything you would tell the adjuster out loud — that you already met, that he paid one shingle and passed over three slopes, that the ridge was walked twice.
