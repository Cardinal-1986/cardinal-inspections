Your "2 years" comment broke something bigger than the form

If a bid can sit two years waiting on a grant cycle, then the app has one clock where it needs two. I measured what a bid submitted in Aug 2024 looks like today:

bid_due_at 2024-08-15 → days() = −713
hub Due column     : "−713 d"
chDueBand()         : "Overdue"
aging sort          : ranks it your most urgent job, forever

bid_due_at is when you promised the partner a price. It is not the award horizon. Once a bid is submitted the screen should age against when to check back — otherwise every long grant turns into permanent red noise and the overdue filter I built today becomes useless.

There is also no hold stage in the vocabulary — Bid Requested, Bid Submitted, Awarded, Scheduled, Build Complete, Invoiced, Closed, Not Awarded. A two-year wait has nowhere to sit but "Bid Submitted".

01Step 1 — the decision

Style 2's flow: four options, each explaining itself, glove-sized. Note the order — Still waiting is second, because on your data it is the most common outcome, not the exception.

AwardedRecords the approved amount and who funded it
Still waitingParks it with a check-back date. Months or years.
Referred onwardMoves the funder. Rebuild, Community Action, County Corp.
Not awardedCloses it. No reason asked.
Habitat sits first in every partner list — you do the majority of their roofs and you're in the annual partnership commercial. It's the anchor relationship, so it shouldn't be alphabetical filler.
